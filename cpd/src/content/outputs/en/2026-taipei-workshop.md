## Background

The CPD methodology document maintains a register of harmonisation items on which the three teams have not reached consensus. By mid-2026 the register held seven entries.

Most projects absorb such disagreements internally or settle them by majority. We chose public deliberation, for a simple reason: these disagreements are value judgements rather than technical disputes. An internal vote does not make the answer more correct — it only makes the disagreement invisible.

## Format

Two days. For each item, the competing positions were each given 15 minutes, followed by 30 minutes of questioning from those present: 11 external researchers, four industry representatives and three environmental NGO representatives. Proceedings were recorded and published.

## Outcome

Three of the seven converged. Two because external researchers produced statistical documents none of the teams held; one because the position was withdrawn during questioning.

The remaining four stand, with the points clarified during deliberation now added to the record. One — whether emissions displaced by offshoring should be netted out of national accounts — was reclassified: on reflection we judged it a political rather than a technical question, and moved it out of the harmonisation ruleset into the discussion of findings.

## An unexpected result

An industry representative pointed out during questioning that our capacity data for some sites uses design rather than operating capacity, which understates emissions per unit of product. Nine sites are affected; the correction is scheduled for dataset v2.2.

**This is what open deliberation is for.** None of the three teams held that operational knowledge.
