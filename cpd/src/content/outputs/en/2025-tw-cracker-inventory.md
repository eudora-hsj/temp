## Summary

This report reconstructs the 2005–2024 emissions series for 14 naphtha cracking and major derivative chemical sites in Taiwan, applying the CPD harmonisation rules and comparing the result against Ministry of Environment greenhouse gas statistics.

## A result requiring careful reading

Absolute emissions from the Taiwanese petrochemical sector have fallen since 2018, by roughly 11.4%. Taken alone, that reads as substantial progress.

Disaggregated, ethylene output fell 8.9% over the same period. Measured per unit of product, actual efficiency improvement is around 2.8%, concentrated in the few sites that completed equipment replacement. **Most of the reduction comes from lower output, not from improved process performance.**

This is not in itself a criticism of policy — capacity adjustment is a form of transition. But it does mean the gain is not secure if output recovers. Targets defined against absolute emissions will systematically mislead assessment in cases of this kind.

## Data and method

Sources are the Ministry of Environment's GHG reporting registry, company annual reports and environmental monitoring records. On-site generation emissions are moved from the energy sector back into manufacturing under CPD rules, raising the sector total by about 8.3%.

Site-level data appears in the project's open dataset, with fields touching undisclosed commercial detail given as ranges.
