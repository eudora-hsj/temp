## The problem

Comparison tables for petrochemical decarbonization circulate freely in policy debate, drawn from figures each country publishes on its own statistical basis — and those bases do not agree.

We identify three principal gaps: **sectoral scope** (whether downstream derivative plants are included varies considerably), **attribution of on-site generation** (Taiwan to energy, the UK to manufacturing, Japan according to firm-level reporting), and **Scope 2 baselines** (grid emission factors updated at different intervals).

## Method

We reviewed reported data for 41 sites from 2005 to 2024 and built a three-layer set of conversion rules. Every adjustment retains the pre-adjustment value, the delta and the rule code, so third parties can work backwards and apply their own rules instead.

The rules were cross-checked by all three teams. Seven items where consensus was not reached are listed in the methodology document under "unresolved disagreements" and flagged in the dataset.

## Principal findings

After harmonisation, national totals diverge from official statistics by +8.3% (Taiwan), −2.1% (UK) and +11.7% (Japan). The largest single contributor is on-site generation attribution — enough on its own to reorder the three countries by reduction achieved.

The change in trend matters more. On official statistics, the 2015–2024 fall in emissions per unit of product ranks UK > Japan > Taiwan. After harmonisation the gap between Japan and the UK narrows from 6.2 to 1.4 percentage points, inside the range of data uncertainty. **A difference cited to argue the superiority of one policy instrument turns out to be substantially an artefact of measurement.**

## Limitation

Our harmonisation rules are one defensible set of choices, not the only correct answer. We publish the rules and the original values precisely so that researchers who disagree can recompute.
