## Why we started

In 2023, three official reports on petrochemical decarbonization — Taiwanese, British and Japanese — were presented side by side at an international meeting. All three claimed progress against target. When participants tried to overlay the curves, the discussion stalled: nobody could confirm the figures measured the same thing.

Taiwan assigns emissions from on-site generation to the energy sector, the UK to manufacturing, Japan according to how each firm reports. Scope 2 baselines differ too. Cross-national comparison tables circulate widely, yet most rest on assumptions no reader can check.

CPD begins there. We do not argue that one country's pathway is more successful. We build the basis on which such a claim could be tested.

## How we work

The study covers 41 naphtha cracking and derivative chemical sites across the three countries, spanning 2005 to 2024, in three stages:

1. **Boundary harmonisation** — site-by-site review of industrial classification, on-site generation and Scope 2 methods, producing traceable conversion rules. Every adjustment retains its original value so third parties can work backwards.
2. **Policy mapping** — carbon prices, subsidies and transition funds set against the timing of actual capital expenditure, drawn from annual reports, environmental assessments and disclosed investment records.
3. **Social cost assessment** — pollution exposure, employment structure and local fiscal dependence around six representative sites.

Interim findings are cross-checked at joint meetings. In practice the sharpest arguments always concern stage one: each team tends to regard its own national conventions as the reasonable baseline.

## A limitation we state plainly

Our harmonisation rules are a set of defensible choices, not neutral truth. We publish every rule alongside its rationale and the alternatives considered, so that others can recompute. If your results differ from ours, that is what the dataset is for.

## Data and licensing

The harmonised site-level dataset is released under CC BY 4.0 with conversion rules, original values and a list of known limitations. Fields touching undisclosed commercial detail are withheld, but the methodology document marks where those gaps fall and what they may affect.
